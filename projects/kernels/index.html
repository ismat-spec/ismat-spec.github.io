Convolution Kernel Lab

Every image filter — and the first layer of every convolutional neural network — is a tiny grid of numbers slid across the picture. Pick a preset, or edit the 3×3 kernel yourself and watch the image change instantly. Blur, sharpen and edge detection are all just different numbers in the grid.

Applied in –

The kernel

Sum 1 · divisor 1 · offset 0
Each output pixel = its 3×3 neighbourhood, multiplied cell-by-cell by this grid, summed, divided by the divisor, plus the offset.

What does this have to do with AI?

A convolutional neural network (the architecture behind modern image recognition) is built from exactly this operation. The difference is that nobody chooses the numbers: the network learns thousands of kernels from data, and the early ones typically converge to edge and texture detectors remarkably similar to the Sobel and Laplacian presets here. Playing with this lab is playing with what a CNN's first layer does — by hand. Notice also that blur kernels sum to 1 (they average), while edge kernels sum to 0 (flat areas go black: no edge, no signal).
